Our story

Why I started Lateral G Therapy

Peter Gerbracht, founder. Air National Guard veteran (20 years). Racer. Survivor of suicide loss.

Draft for review

I spent 20 years in the Air National Guard. I came home from a deployment to Iraq with habits I still carry. When I walk into a room, I find the exits. In a crowd, I watch the faces.

Then I lost someone I love to suicide. Nothing prepares you for that kind of grief: the questions without answers, the weight that's there every morning. For a while, I didn't know where to put any of it.

What helped came in three parts. First, professional help. Therapy gave me real tools for the hardest moments. Second, people who understood. At a support group for survivors of suicide loss, nobody had exactly my story, but they knew exactly how it felt, and that was a relief. Third, I found racing.

Behind the wheel, there's only room for the next corner. At autocross, I walk the course four times and rehearse every turn before I drive it. The same watchfulness that makes a crowd hard is an advantage on course. When the car loads up in a hard corner, you feel the lateral G's, and you have to commit. For those minutes, the grief takes a back seat. And in the paddock, I found people who welcomed me without needing me to explain anything.

That's where the name comes from. Lateral G Therapy isn't a replacement for therapy. It's what I found alongside it: real help, real people, and an outlet. I started it so that men, veterans, and families who've lost someone don't have to find those things alone, or the hard way.

— Peter
